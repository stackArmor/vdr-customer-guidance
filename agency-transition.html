Agency coordination / VDR and VER transition

Keep your agency
in the conversation.

Discuss the transition with your agency contacts now: what they expect to receive, how they will interpret the new evaluations, and which processes must continue for your authorization.

Start with the receiving agency

Agree on how the transition will work.

FedRAMP’s published adoption date for VDR and VER is December 7, 2026, aligned with CISA BOD 26-04. Ask your agency contacts how they are preparing their review and reporting processes for that transition and what they need from your team. Coordinate expectations against the published milestone.

For FedCiv customers, reporting options under consideration support the VDR / VER transition. They do not represent an offer to maintain a separate traditional CVSS-based POA&M process. The continued legacy workflow described below applies to DoD/DoW workloads.

Useful questions for the agency’s authorization and continuous-monitoring contacts include:

  • How are you preparing to receive and review VDR / VER evaluations and reporting?
  • Which VDR / VER reports and supporting evidence will your reviewers use, and would a familiar tabular view of those results help?
  • How should we explain PAIN ratings, exploitation likelihood, internet reachability, and the resulting remediation dates to your reviewers?
  • How should historical asset identifiers and findings be correlated with the VDR reports?
  • Who should resolve reporting questions, and how should we communicate changes and exceptions?

Share this guidance and the evaluation-method explanation with your agency contacts so the discussion starts from the same information.

Continued support for DoD/DoW workloads

The existing workflow remains available.

stackArmor will keep the existing vulnerability management workflow functional for customers managing DoD/DoW workloads. The VDR transition does not mean that workflow will be disabled for those customers.

If a system supports both DoD/DoW and FedRAMP workloads, you may need to manage two vulnerability processes: the existing process required by your DoD/DoW sponsor and the VDR / VER process for FedRAMP. This may be necessary during the transition and could continue longer. The duration and scope depend on the requirements and guidance of the applicable authorities.

Engage your DoD/DoW sponsor directly. Ask whether it supports VDR-based evaluation or reporting for your authorization, whether updated guidance is available or under discussion, and which existing requirements it expects you to continue following. Your sponsor may have information about the direction of those discussions that is not yet available to stackArmor.

Share the sponsor’s response with your stackArmor team so we can help coordinate the appropriate workflows and reporting.

An option under consideration

A companion view of VDR results.

stackArmor is exploring a tabular companion report alongside applicable structured VDR JSON reports. It could use familiar POA&M-style columns to help agency reviewers interpret VDR findings. This would be a presentation of the VDR evaluation and reporting process, not support for a separate traditional POA&M workflow for FedCiv agencies. The companion view remains under consideration.

Such a report would be fundamentally different from the traditional POA&M because it would represent VDR-based findings and decisions:

Report elementWhat agency reviewers should expect
Asset identifiersIdentifiers may change as findings are associated with the workload or configuration component that must be updated for a lasting fix. Discuss how reviewers should correlate affected resources between reports.
Impact and severityEvaluated impact would reflect PAIN and agency / workload context. A PAIN rating is not a direct conversion of a raw CVSS severity.
Remediation timingDue dates would follow the applicable VDR determination using PAIN, Certification Class, exploitation likelihood, and internet reachability, with applicable KEV deadlines also considered. These can differ from traditional CVSS-based timelines.
Supporting contextThe report would need to explain the PAIN / LEV / IRV decisions and provide references back to the underlying findings and evidence.

Ask the agency which fields, identifiers, or explanations would help its reviewers understand the VDR results. A familiar report layout can help communication while preserving the meaning of the new evaluation.

Help shape the transition

Bring agency questions and suggestions back to us.

stackArmor is exploring reporting and communication options to help customers explain VDR results and maintain trust with their agencies during this transition. We welcome suggestions that improve understanding of the new process.

Share sponsor guidance, requested report formats, unclear mappings, and suggestions through your engagement manager or shared stackArmor Slack channel. We can discuss the options, identify what is practical, and update this guidance as common questions emerge.

For the underlying approach, see the eight evaluation factors, the detailed methodology, and the engineering guide. You can subscribe to guidance updates as the transition develops.
