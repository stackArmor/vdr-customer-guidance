System context

Only you know what a workload means to your system.

Under VDR and VER, PAIN evaluates a vulnerability in the context of the affected workload or cloud resource—what data flows through it, what privileges or administrative authority it holds, what agency mission function depends on it, and whether compromise could affect one agency or several (multiAgency).

stackArmor operates TSW, deploys the supporting scanning infrastructure, and maintains the scoring pipeline, but we cannot invent your system’s business context on your behalf. Two services or databases that look identical from cloud or cluster metadata can play completely different roles inside your offering. Classification always has to be evaluated within the context of the system, by the people who build and operate it.

What the labels mean

Plain-language impact: exposure, tampering, and downtime.

Security-impact profiles record how important confidentiality, integrity, and availability are for each workload or resource. Each dimension answers a concrete question about what happens if that asset is compromised:

RequirementWhat you are deciding
Confidentiality (CR)How sensitive is the data, credential set, or administrative access on this workload—and how severe would the impact be if it were exposed to the internet or an unauthorized party?
Integrity (IR)What would the impact be if a bad actor made unauthorized changes—manipulating, forging, or corrupting data, configuration, or actions coming from this workload?
Availability (AR)What would the impact be if this workload or its data went down and were unavailable to the government?

This connects directly to FedRAMP’s Minimum Assessment Scope (MAS) rules under the consolidated / 20x framework (such as MAS-CSO-IIR and MAS-CSO-FLO): every information resource inside the Cloud Service Offering boundary that handles federal data or can impact its confidentiality, integrity, or availability must be identified with documented information flows and security categories. Security-impact profiles put that boundary context into a consistent, machine-readable form for VDR scoring.

Expedited drafting, human accountability

AI handles the first pass. Your team owns the outcome.

Classifying every workload and cloud resource can be real work, especially the first time through. That is why stackArmor created the open-source VDR agent skills: to run read-only discovery across your clusters and cloud accounts and make strong first-pass judgment calls in minutes instead of days or weeks.

Even when an approved AI assistant drafts a strong baseline, AI can still miss system-specific nuances, compensating architecture, or mission context. A team could theoretically rubber-stamp whatever the agent outputs, but those profiles directly drive PAIN tiers and compliance remediation timelines. Your engineers and mission owners should review the drafts, overrule anything that does not match reality, and understand and own those outcomes as the system evolves.

Built for day-two operations

Group workloads centrally or label resources directly.

We built multiple ways to manage assignments so teams do not have to manually update classification metadata every time a service changes or scales:

  • Central grouping and pattern rules: In the Kubernetes vdr-fedramp ConfigMap or vdr-cloud.yaml, you can group workloads and cloud resources by namespace, name prefix, supported regular expressions, resource type, or existing cloud tags. New assets matching an established rule inherit coverage automatically.
  • Direct labels on workloads or cloud resources: You can also apply VDR labels or tags directly on Kubernetes namespaces and workloads or in cloud infrastructure definitions (such as Helm values or Terraform) when you want profiles maintained alongside the code.
  • Additive maintenance skills: When new assets appear or roles shift, update-k8s-vdr-configmap and update-cloud-vdr-config compare current inventory against existing assignments and draft additive updates without redoing reviewed profiles. See how coverage maintenance works.

Better signal and industry practice

Stronger engineering signal—and a shared FedRAMP reality.

Beyond satisfying VDR rules, owning this classification improves vulnerability management across your engineering organization. Teams come away with a clear map of which workloads carry the highest mission impact and a deeper understanding of how CVSS subscores, internet reachability, and PAIN work together—turning severity ratings from opaque numbers into clear, defensible remediation priorities.

From stackArmor’s participation in the CSP Advisory Board (CSP-AB) and ongoing conversations with fellow cloud service providers and 3PAOs, every organization implementing FedRAMP VDR / VER—whether on its own, with stackArmor, or through other compliance accelerators—is doing a version of this workload metadata labeling. No scanner or compliance provider can compute contextual agency impact without system-owner input.
